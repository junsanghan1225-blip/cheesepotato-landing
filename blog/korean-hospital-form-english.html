Filling Out a Korean Hospital Intake Form: Symptoms, History, and Prescriptions

  • Life in Korea
  • English

Seeking medical care in a foreign country can be intimidating, especially when illness strikes suddenly and medical terminology feels unfamiliar. Fortunately, South Korea boasts one of the most accessible and efficient healthcare systems in the world. Walk-in consultations at neighborhood primary care clinics (의원) require no weeks-long waitlists, and consultations are remarkably affordable under National Health Insurance.

Upon arriving at an internal medicine clinic (내과), orthopedic center (정형외과), or ENT specialist (이비인후과), the reception desk will hand you a first-visit questionnaire known as a 문진표 (medical intake questionnaire). Filling out this document accurately ensures that attending physicians diagnose your condition swiftly without prescribing contraindicated drugs. Below is an annotated breakdown of a standard Korean clinic intake form.

At reception: Insurance verification and clinic tiers

In South Korea, medical institutions are organized into three tiers: primary neighborhood clinics (1차 의원), general secondary hospitals (2차 병원), and tertiary university medical centers (3차 상급종합병원). For colds, mild sprains, skin rashes, or digestive issues, visit a local neighborhood clinic first. You do not need a prior appointment; simply walk in during clinic operating hours and present your Alien Registration Card (외국인등록증) or mobile health insurance pass.

The receptionist checks your national health database record within seconds. Under statutory National Health Insurance (국민건강보험), patients typically pay only 20 to 30 percent of primary consultation costs out of pocket, with basic visits often costing less than 10,000 KRW including diagnostic tests. If you require specialized tertiary hospital care, you must first obtain a referral slip (진료의뢰서) from a primary clinic to qualify for insurance coverage.

Describing pain and symptoms with precise Korean terms

Korean medical vocabulary offers rich, descriptive nuances for physical discomfort. When speaking to the physician or filling out the intake form, choosing the right adjective makes an immediate difference. For headaches or muscle aches, 욱신거리다 describes a throbbing, pulsating sensation, while 지끈거리다 captures a splitting tension headache. If pain feels like sharp needles pricking your skin or nerves, use 콕콕 쑤시다 or 찌릿하다.

For gastrointestinal distress, 속이 메스껍다 signifies nausea, whereas 속이 쓰리다 indicates burning acid reflux or stomach irritation. If your joints or back feel heavy and stiff, doctors recognize 뻐근하다 or 묵직하다. When tracking fever, tell the doctor whether chills accompanied the temperature (오한이 들다) and whether the fever responded to over-the-counter antipyretics.

Drug safety and the national DUR checking network

When the doctor inputs your prescription into their clinical software, Korea's nationwide Drug Utilization Review (DUR) system automatically audits the selected compounds against your prescription history across all clinics in real time. The DUR system flags dangerous duplicate ingredients or harmful pharmacological interactions before the doctor prints the order.

However, the electronic DUR network cannot track over-the-counter remedies, imported medications, or undocumented drug allergies. That is why completing Section 6 on your intake form is vital. If you ever experienced hives (두드러기), facial swelling (부종), or shortness of breath (호흡 곤란) after taking antibiotics or pain relievers, make sure the physician records the allergy on your permanent clinic profile.

Receiving your prescription and visiting the pharmacy

At the conclusion of your consultation, the clinic desk issues two copies of your medical prescription (처방전)—one marked for the dispensing pharmacy (약국 제출용) and one for your personal records (환자 보관용). Take the pharmacy copy to any dispensary located right outside the clinic or nearby.

Korean pharmacies pack your tablets into transparent individualized dose packets (약포지) labeled with morning, afternoon, or evening icons. The paper medicine bag (약봉투) doubles as an official legal receipt and drug manual, detailing the active chemical names of each pill, photographic identification guides, and dietary recommendations such as taking doses 30 minutes after meals (식후 30분).

Claiming private insurance: Documents to request

If you hold private Korean indemnity medical insurance (실손의료보험) or overseas travel health coverage, you can recover a major portion of your out-of-pocket clinic and pharmacy costs. Before leaving the clinic, ask the front desk for an itemized medical expense receipt (진료비 영수증) and a detailed calculation statement (진료비 세부내역서). Both documents are issued free of charge upon request.

For small claims under 100,000 KRW, most domestic insurance mobile apps allow you to snap photos of your pharmacy medicine bag and clinic receipt to receive reimbursements within 24 to 48 hours without submitting physical doctor certificates.
