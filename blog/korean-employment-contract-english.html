Reading a Korean Employment Contract: Key Clauses and Common Traps

  • Life in Korea
  • English

Every international resident working in South Korea must sign a written employment contract before beginning work. Under Article 17 of Korea's Labor Standards Act (근로기준법), employers are legally obligated to issue a signed duplicate copy detailing mandatory working conditions directly to the employee. Verbal promises made during interviews or informal hiring chats carry virtually no weight in wage disputes, labor arbitration hearings, or immigration audits.

For workers holding E-9 non-professional employment visas, E-7 specialized occupation visas, D-10 job seeker visas, F-visa family residents, or working holiday visas, the standard employment contract (표준근로계약서) serves as the legal bedrock for your lawful stay and labor rights. Before picking up a pen or stamping your personal seal (도장), review the document line by line against the annotated example below.

Contracted hours versus overtime: The 209-hour standard

In South Korea, standard full-time employment is legally defined as 40 hours per week (8 hours per day, 5 days per week). When calculating monthly salaries, the Ministry of Employment and Labor adds 8 weekly paid holiday hours to the 40 worked hours, totaling 48 paid hours per week. Multiplying 48 hours by the average 4.345 weeks in a month yields the famous 209-hour standard.

Any labor performed beyond your 8 daily contracted hours constitutes overtime work (연장근로). Overtime, night work performed between 22:00 and 06:00 (야간근로), and holiday shifts (휴일근로) must each be compensated with an extra 50 percent premium above your ordinary hourly wage (통상임금). Total combined overtime cannot exceed 12 hours per week under Korea's 52-hour statutory workweek cap. Keep your own calendar log of punch-in and punch-out stamps to compare against your monthly pay slip.

Weekly holiday allowance: Demystifying 주휴수당

Many foreign employees are surprised to find extra paid hours on their contract. Under Article 55 of the Labor Standards Act, any worker who logs at least 15 scheduled hours per week and completes all agreed shifts must receive at least one paid day off every week. This statutory entitlement is called 주휴수당 (weekly holiday allowance).

If an employer pays by the hour or day instead of a fixed monthly salary, verify whether the quoted hourly rate already bundles weekly holiday pay or excludes it. If your contract states an hourly wage without clear notation, your employer must calculate and add weekly holiday hours whenever you maintain full attendance during the working week. Clear documentation prevents wage shortfalls at the end of the month.

Annual paid leave entitlements (연차유급휴가)

Another crucial right frequently overlooked is statutory annual paid leave under Article 60 of the Labor Standards Act. During your first year of employment, you accrue one day of paid vacation for every month of perfect attendance, up to a maximum of 11 days. After completing your first full year with at least 80 percent attendance, you receive 15 days of paid annual leave. If you do not use these days due to business needs, unused leave must be compensated as cash allowance upon contract conclusion.

Probation periods and legal wage limits

Employers frequently request a probation period (수습기간). Korean labor law permits companies to pay 90 percent of the statutory minimum wage during probation, but only under strict legal criteria: the total contract duration must be one year or longer, and the reduced rate cannot apply for more than three months. For simple manual labor roles designated by national occupational standards, no wage deduction is permitted even during probation. If your contract is for ten or eleven months, any probation discount is completely unlawful.

Dormitory deductions and severance pay safeguards

A common point of dispute for overseas workers involves housing and utilities deductions. Under Ministry guidelines, employers cannot unilaterally withdraw money from your pay packet for food or dormitory accommodation. Any deduction requires a separate written consent form (기숙사비 공제 동의서) clearly stating the accommodation type, facilities provided, and itemized monthly fees. Temporary container units or makeshift structures have strict deduction caps and safety standards.

Furthermore, every worker who remains with an enterprise for one full year or longer is entitled to statutory severance pay (퇴직금), equivalent to at least 30 days of average wages for each consecutive year of service. For E-9 workers, employers are mandated to deposit monthly premiums into Departure Guarantee Insurance (출국만기보험). If the accumulated insurance fund upon departure falls short of your legal severance entitlement, the employer is legally obligated to settle the remaining balance in cash before you leave the country.
