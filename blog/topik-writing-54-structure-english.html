TOPIK writing Q54: how to structure the essay

한국어로 읽기 →
  • English
  • TOPIK

Question 54 of the TOPIK II exam asks you to write an argumentative essay of 600 to 700 characters. Worth 50 points, it frequently determines whether a test taker achieves Level 4 or Level 6. Scorers routinely penalise essays that have zero spelling mistakes simply because the underlying paragraph structure lacks logical coherence.

Because time pressure in the writing section is intense, memorising a predictable structural framework before exam day is the single most reliable way to score 40 points or higher.

Every prompt contains two or three sub-questions

Under the main topic description, the test sheet lists two or three bullet points. Treat these bullet points as your paragraph blueprint. Dedicating exactly one body paragraph to each bullet point guarantees you answer every required grading criterion.

If a prompt asks: 1. Why is lifelong learning necessary? 2. What difficulties do adults face when learning? 3. What measures are needed to support them? — your essay must follow this exact sequence across paragraphs 2, 3, and 4.

Introduction: Do not copy the prompt

The single most common deduction in the introduction is copying sentences directly from the question sheet. Scorers disregard copied lines when calculating total character count. Open your essay by framing why the issue matters in your own words in two or three sentences.

Common mistakeParaphrasing only one particle from the prompt still counts as plagiarising the question prompt. Always introduce the topic using fresh vocabulary.

A reliable formula for your opening sentence is stating a broad social phenomenon: 「최근 우리 사회에서는 ~에 대한 관심이 높아지고 있다」 (Recently in our society, interest in ~ has been rising) or 「현대 사회에서 ~은 중요한 문제로 부각되고 있다」 (In modern society, ~ is emerging as a critical issue).

Body paragraphs: One claim plus evidence

Never list bare opinions without justification. Structure each body paragraph with a clear opening claim followed by concrete reasoning or real-world examples.

Connect your ideas using written transition words like 「이에 따라」 (accordingly), 「반면에」 (on the other hand), and 「따라서」 (therefore), rather than conversational fillers like 그리고 or 그래서.

Conclusion: Synthesise without introducing new arguments

Your conclusion should briefly summarise your core arguments and offer a forward-looking recommendation or social call to action. Never introduce an entirely new argument in the final sentence.

Close with formal necessity structures such as 「-(으)ㄹ 필요가 있다」 (there is a need to), 「-(으)려고 노력해야 한다」 (efforts must be made to), or 「-는 것이 바람직하다」 (it is desirable that).

Use the plain written style (-는다 / -다)

Never write Question 54 in conversational polite styles (해요체 or 합니다체). Academic essays must be written in the plain narrative style (해라체), ending in 「-는다/다」, 「-(으)ㄹ 필요가 있다」, or 「-는 것이 바람직하다」.

  • Action verbs: -(느)ㄴ다 (생각한다, 발생한다, 증가한다)
  • Descriptive adjectives: -다 without ㄴ/는 (중요하다, 심각하다, 필요하다)
  • Copula nouns: -이다 / -가 아니다 (문제이다, 원인이 아니다)
  • Past tense: -았다/었다 (나타났다, 발전했다)
  • Future and conjecture: -(으)ㄹ 것이다 / -(으)ㄹ 것으로 보인다

Mastering the manuscript grid (원고지) layout

Writing on manuscript paper follows precise spacing rules that examiners inspect during grading. Violating basic grid formatting costs valuable points even when your text is well written.

  • Indent the very first square of every new paragraph by exactly one blank space
  • Never leave a blank square at the start of normal continuing lines inside a paragraph
  • Place periods, commas, and quotation marks in the bottom-left corner of their own square
  • Write Arabic numbers and Latin letters two per square (e.g. '20' or 'AI' share a single box)
  • If punctuation falls at the end of a line, write it in the margin rather than starting a new line with a period

Time allocation strategy for the 50-minute writing section

The TOPIK II writing test allows exactly 50 minutes for Questions 51 through 54. Running out of time on Question 54 is the number one cause of failed score goals.

  1. Minutes 0 to 10: Complete Question 51 and Question 52 (fill-in-the-blank short questions)
  2. Minutes 10 to 25: Complete Question 53 (data and graph description, 200 to 300 characters)
  3. Minutes 25 to 30: Outline Question 54 (brainstorm keywords and assign bullet points to paragraphs)
  4. Minutes 30 to 48: Write Question 54 steadily on the manuscript grid
  5. Minutes 48 to 50: Final proofread for missing particles, spelling errors, and plain-style verb endings

Frequently asked questions

What happens if I write fewer than 600 characters?

The official prompt sets the target length at 600 to 700 characters. If your essay stops short of 600 characters, scorers apply tiered deductions. Essays below 500 characters lose substantial points, and writing under 400 characters risks receiving a zero on the task achievement rubric.

Can I use first-person pronouns like 나 or 저?

Avoid 「나」, 「저」, and 「우리」 in formal essays. Instead of writing 「제 생각에는」 (In my opinion), phrase your stance objectively: 「~라고 볼 수 있다」 (It can be viewed that ~) or 「~라는 점에 주목해야 한다」 (We must pay attention to the fact that ~). Refer to society as 「현대 사회의 구성원」 or 「사람들」 rather than our group.

Should I write a separate title on the top line of the grid?

No. Unlike Korean school writing contests, TOPIK Question 54 does not require a title. Begin immediately on the first line with an indented square and your opening introduction sentence. Writing a title wastes valuable manuscript space.

Quick self-check quiz

Check your knowledge of TOPIK Question 54 essay rules:

  1. 1. 원고지에서 문단이 바뀔 때 첫 칸은: (비워 둔다 / 채워 쓴다)
  2. 2. 학술 논술문 종결 어미로 알맞은 것은: (중요합니다 / 중요하다 / 중요해요)
  3. 3. 본론 문단을 구성할 때 가장 좋은 기준은: (문제에 제시된 소주제 질문들 / 떠오르는 생각 순서)
  4. 4. 서론을 작성할 때 피해야 할 것은: (문제지 지문 문장 그대로 베껴 쓰기 / 화제 제시하기)
