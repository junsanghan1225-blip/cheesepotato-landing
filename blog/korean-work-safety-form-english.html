Reading a Korean Workplace Safety Checklist: PPE, Hazards, and Stop-Work Rights

  • Life in Korea
  • English

Every morning across South Korea's manufacturing plants, construction sites, and agricultural facilities, teams gather for a pre-shift safety briefing known as TBM (Tool Box Meeting). Before heavy presses cycle, cranes lift, or chemicals flow, supervisors present a daily safety checklist and work permit (일일 작업안전 점검표). Signing this document signifies that hazards have been surveyed, protective gear is fitted, and machinery safeguards are functioning.

For migrant workers holding E-9 non-professional visas, E-7 technical visas, or H-2 work permits, understanding these safety sheets is literally a matter of life and limb. Korean labor law establishes strict industrial protections that prioritize worker health above production quotas. Below is an annotated breakdown of a standard Korean industrial safety checklist.

Mandatory PPE: Your employer's legal obligation

Under Article 38 of the Occupational Safety and Health Act (산업안전보건법), employers are legally mandated to furnish personal protective equipment (개인보호구) suited to your specific assignment at zero cost to you. If a manager demands that you buy your own steel-toed boots (안전화) or hard hat (안전모) out of your monthly paycheck, that deduction is illegal.

Different tasks require specific safety ratings. High-impact overhead environments require a certified hard hat with the chin strap tightly buckled (턱끈 체결). Metal-cutting and woodworking stations mandate safety goggles (보안경) and dust respirators (방진마스크). When handling organic solvents or acidic cleaners, chemical-resistant rubber gauntlets and vapor respirators (방독마스크) are strictly required.

Handling hazardous chemicals and MSDS sheets

In paint shops, electroplating facilities, plastic molding lines, and agricultural greenhouses, workers handle volatile industrial reagents and toxic solvents. Under Korea's chemical safety laws, every container holding hazardous substances must display a GHS pictographic warning label indicating flammability, toxicity, or corrosive properties.

Furthermore, a comprehensive Material Safety Data Sheet (물질안전보건자료 or MSDS) must be posted in direct sight of the workstation. The MSDS outlines first-aid measures in the event of skin contact or inhalation, proper fire suppression chemicals, and required ventilation procedures. Before opening any chemical vat, inspect the nearest emergency eyewash station (비상 세안기) to confirm water pressure.

The deadly risk of machine jams and the LOTO protocol

The single most frequent fatal accident type in Korean manufacturing plants is the entanglement or pinch accident (끼임·협착 사고). These devastating injuries occur almost exclusively when an operator attempts to dislodge a jammed workpiece, clear metal shavings, or adjust a conveyor belt while the electric motor is energized.

Korean safety regulations demand absolute adherence to the LOTO (Lockout / Tagout) principle. Whenever entering the machine perimeter for maintenance, power switches must be shut down, locked with a physical padlock, and labeled with a warning sign stating 'Machine Under Inspection' (점검 중 조작 금지). Never bypass or tape over infrared light curtains (방호장치) to speed up assembly cycles.

Exercising your legal right to stop dangerous work

Under Article 52 of the Occupational Safety and Health Act, every worker in South Korea possesses the statutory Right to Stop Work (작업중지권). If you observe an imminent risk of industrial catastrophe—such as unstable scaffolding in high winds, damaged electrical wiring near puddles, defective machine brakes, or toxic gas leaks—you have the legal authority to halt operations immediately and evacuate to a safe zone.

The law explicitly protects workers who invoke this right: employers are prohibited from firing, disciplining, withholding wages, or discriminating against any employee who stops work due to reasonable safety fears. Immediately report the hazardous condition to your company safety officer (안전관리자) or union representative.

Industrial accident insurance (산재보험) covers everyone

If an injury occurs on the job or while commuting directly between home and factory, you are fully shielded by Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance (산업재해보상보험). This state-backed insurance covers 100 percent of hospital treatment, rehabilitation therapy, and pays 70 percent of your average daily wage throughout your temporary disability leave (휴업급여).

Crucially, Korean industrial accident insurance applies regardless of fault, regardless of whether your employer consents, and regardless of your visa status. Even undocumented foreign workers are fully eligible for industrial accident compensation without being handed over to immigration authorities during medical recovery.
