N이/가 있다·없다	Says whether something exists or not. Also used for having something.<br>N + 이/가 있어요 · 없어요<br>시간이 있어요?	grammar-beginner

시간	Hours are read in native Korean numbers, while minutes are read in Sino-Korean numbers.<br>Native Korean number + 「시」 + Sino-Korean number + 「분」<br>세 시 삼십 분에 만나요.	grammar-beginner
A/V-(스)ㅂ니다	The most formal speech style. Used in presentations, meetings, news broadcasts, and when meeting someone for the first time.<br>Verb/Adj stem without final consonant + 「-ㅂ니다」 / with final consonant + 「-습니다」<br>저는 한국어를 공부합니다.	grammar-beginner

V-(으)ㄹ 거예요 ①	Used to talk about future actions or plans that have already been decided.<br>Verb stem without final consonant + 「-ㄹ 거예요」 / with final consonant + 「-을 거예요」<br>내일 도서관에 갈 거예요.	grammar-beginner

N을/를	Object marker indicating the target of an action.<br>Noun with final consonant + 「을」 / without final consonant + 「를」<br>저녁마다 한국 드라마를 봐요.	grammar-beginner

N에게/한테	Marks the person you give something to or say something to.<br>에게 (written) · 한테 (spoken) · 께 (honorific)<br>친구한테 선물을 줬어요.	grammar-beginner

N(이)나 ① (선택)	Particle expressing an option between choices, meaning 'or'.<br>Noun with final consonant + 「이나」 / without final consonant + 「나」<br>커피나 차를 드릴까요?	grammar-beginner
N(이)나 ② (많음)	Attached to numbers to express surprise that an amount is more than expected.<br>Number + 「(이)나」<br>세 시간이나 기다렸어요.	grammar-beginner
