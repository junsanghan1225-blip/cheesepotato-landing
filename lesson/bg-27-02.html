열쇠 하나였다 — -(으)ㄴ / -는 / -(으)ㄹ · 8분

Take a deep breath and look back at your journey from Stage 2 through Stage 5. You learned so many essential Korean expressions:

- -(으)ㄹ 거예요 *(Future: I will do)*
- -(으)ㄹ 때 *(Time: When I do)*
- -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 *(Ability: I can do)*
- -(으)ㄹ까요 *(Question / Suggestion: Shall we?)*
- -(으)ㄹ게요 *(Promise / Intention: I will)*
- -(으)ㄴ 후에 *(Sequence: After doing)*
- -(으)ㄴ 적이 있다 *(Experience: I have done)*
- -는 것 같다 *(Conjecture: It seems like)*

Did it feel like Korean was an endless sea of separate, complicated endings to memorize? Prepare to see the entire language in a completely new light.

You did NOT memorize 8 different grammar rules.

Every single one of those expressions is just the Noun-Modifying Form snapping directly onto a noun!

- 때 is literally a noun meaning “time / moment”.
- 수 is a noun meaning “way / ability / means”.
- 후(後) is a noun meaning “after / following”.
- 적 is a noun meaning “past instance / occasion”.
- 것 is a noun meaning “thing / fact / appearance” (and 거예요 is just 것이에요 spoken quickly!).
-(으)ㄹ 거예요 (Future)-(으)ㄹ (future modifier) · 것 (thing) + 이에요 (is) · “It is a thing that will happen”
-(으)ㄹ 때 (When)-(으)ㄹ (future/contingent modifier) · 때 (time / moment) · “At the time of doing”
-(으)ㄹ 수 있다 (Can)-(으)ㄹ (potential modifier) · 수 (way / means) + 있다 (exists) · “A way to do it exists”
-(으)ㄹ까요 (Shall we?)-(으)ㄹ (future modifier) · 까 (question) + 요 (polite) · “Will it happen? / Shall we?”
-(으)ㄹ게요 (I will)-(으)ㄹ (future modifier) · 게요 (promise ending) · “I promise that I will do”
-(으)ㄴ 후에 (After)-(으)ㄴ (past modifier) · 후 (after) + 에 (at) · “At the time after having done”
-(으)ㄴ 적이 있다 (Experience)-(으)ㄴ (past modifier) · 적 (past occasion) + 이 있다 (exists) · “A past instance of doing exists”
-는 것 같다 (Conjecture)-는 (present modifier) · 것 (appearance) + 같다 (is alike) · “It is like the thing happening”

Look at that table again. Everything connects!

- When you said *“갈 거예요”*, you were literally saying [to go + thing + is].
- When you said *“먹을 때”*, you were saying [to eat + time].
- When you said *“한국에 간 적이 있어요”*, you were saying [to Korea + gone + occasion + exists].

Korean isn’t a chaotic web of random endings. It is a breathtakingly logical set of LEGO blocks built on the noun-modifying system!

갈 거예요 = 갈 (modifier) + 것 (thing) + 이에요Future: literally "it is a going-thing"
먹을 때 = 먹을 (modifier) + 때 (time)Time: literally "eating-time"
간 적이 있어요 = 간 (modifier) + 적 (occasion) + 이 있어요Experience: literally "gone-occasion exists"
오는 것 같아요 = 오는 (modifier) + 것 (appearance) + 같아요Conjecture: literally "resembles coming-thing"
